# Plot review

Each plot shows one numeric column for the ten active benchmarks most users follow (BILL4W, BILL13W,
BILL26W, BILL52W, NOTE2Y, NOTE5Y, NOTE10Y, BOND30Y, TIPS10Y, FRN2Y). All 29 were read.

- **Rates** (high, low and median yield, discount rate, investment rate, interest rate): track the known
  history. Bills peak at 15 to 18% in 1980 and 1981, sit at zero in 2009 to 2015 and again in 2020 and
  2021, and climb back above 5% in 2023. TIPS yields go negative in 2011 to 2013 and 2020 to 2021, which
  is real (negative real yields), not a parsing error. No gaps or flat lines inside a benchmark's life.
- **Discount margin and spread** (FRN2Y only, from 2014): small and partly negative in 2022, as
  published by the Treasury.
- **High price**: bills below par by their discount, coupons near par, TIPS above par in the
  low-yield years. Consistent with the rates.
- **Sizes** (offering, tendered, accepted, competitive, noncompetitive): grow steadily, with the bill
  surge of 2020 and 2023 visible. Three points near zero are real $25 million auctions (NOTE10Y
  2019-06-21, FRN2Y 2019-12-06 and 2022-07-14), the small operational auctions the Treasury held in
  those years.
- **Bid-to-cover**: complete from late 1999 (three earlier auctions carry one), mostly 2 to 5. The highest values (9 to
  10.7) are 4-week bills in September and October 2015, when debt-ceiling cuts shrank the offering.
- **Bidder breakdown** (direct, indirect, primary dealers, SOMA, FIMA): starts on 2008-04-07 as
  documented. SOMA bills are constant rollovers in 2008 to 2012 and zero in 2013 to 2019, when the
  Federal Reserve held no bills. FIMA noncompetitive awards cap at 1 billion and then 2 billion from 2023,
  the published limit.
- **Allocation percentage**: uniform between 0 and 100, as expected for the share filled at the stop.
